# MathArc Research v0.2 — Round 1 delivery

## Status

This round converts the v0.2 design into an executable, auditable research runtime on top of the frozen v0.1 contract.

The delivery is an engineering claim, not a claim that any open mathematical problem has been solved. Likewise, MathArc is not labelled “stronger than every existing agent” until the pinned comparison suite is executed under a common budget and all required measurements are present.

## What was learned from the prior mathematical research conversations

The retained lessons are represented as machine-readable failure records and guards rather than informal anecdotes.

1. **Finite verification does not close a universal quantifier.** Exhaustive checks, SAT/SMT UNSAT certificates and large numerical searches may prove a finite or local theorem, but they cannot silently promote a global conjecture.
2. **A useful reduction is not the final theorem.** Compressing a conjecture to one exact obstruction is real progress; the obstruction remains an open load-bearing node until it is independently closed.
3. **Hidden regularity, symmetry and closure assumptions are common failure sources.** Every route records its object types, quantifiers, scope and mechanism fingerprint so that such assumptions can be attacked explicitly.
4. **The generator cannot be its own final judge.** Exact evidence must carry statement correspondence, replay information and, for load-bearing claims, an independent reconstruction path.
5. **Failure is reusable research output.** A falsified route stores its minimal witness, root cause, invalidated descendants, repair and a future guard. Similar later routes are checked against these guards before expensive exploration.
6. **Progress is not probability.** The dashboard separates weighted dependency completion, critical-path closure, engineering acceptance and the binary theorem-closure bit.
7. **A polished manuscript cannot repair a missing bridge.** Release language is generated from verified nodes and open obligations, not from rhetorical confidence.

## Runtime architecture

The runtime contains the following stateful components.

- `TheoremContract`: frozen statement, quantifiers, domain, target scope and acceptance rule.
- `ProofGraph`: acyclic claim/obligation graph with dependency gating and downstream invalidation.
- `RoutePortfolio`: mechanism-distinct research routes with clone detection and diversity metrics.
- `FailureMemory`: persistent failure records, similarity retrieval and reusable guards.
- `PromotionGate`: fail-closed promotion based on dependencies, scope, trust, exactness, statement correspondence and independent replay.
- `PublicReasoningTrace`: objective, premises, proposed move, observation, falsification test and decision. This is the inspectable research trace; private hidden token-level reasoning is not exposed.
- `ToolLedger`: append-only, hash-chained records for commands, inputs, outputs, exit status, artifacts and replay metadata.
- `ResearchLoop`: strategist, falsifier, prover, verifier and synthesizer workers. Worker outputs are proposals and cannot self-promote claims.
- `Dashboard`: proof DAG, critical frontier, route portfolio, failure atlas, tool calls, evidence lineage, claim boundary and guarded baseline comparison.

## Failure-to-repair examples encoded in the demo

The deterministic demo studies the identity

\[
1+3+\cdots +(2n-1)=n^2.
\]

It opens three routes.

- A telescoping route closes the atomic polynomial identity and the finite sum reduction.
- An induction route independently reconstructs the universal result.
- A finite-prefix extrapolation route is deliberately attacked and rejected: checking many values is not accepted as a proof of a universal theorem.

The rejected route is retained as a `FINITE_TO_GLOBAL` failure record and becomes a guard for later research runs.

## Promotion boundary

A claim can reach `VERIFIED` only when all of the following hold.

1. Every dependency is verified.
2. The evidence scope covers the claim scope.
3. The evidence trust level meets the theorem contract.
4. The evidence statement matches the claim statement.
5. Exact claims have exact or formal evidence.
6. Load-bearing claims have an independent replay or reconstruction.
7. No active failure invalidates the claim or an ancestor.

Codex or another language-model worker has proposal authority only. It cannot assign `VERIFIED`, `ACCEPTED` or theorem closure.

## Dashboard metrics

The v0.2 dashboard exposes separate, non-substitutable metrics:

- theorem closure bit;
- weighted dependency completion;
- critical-path closure;
- scope-honesty score;
- evidence-lineage coverage;
- exact replay coverage;
- independent reconstruction coverage;
- public-trace completeness;
- tool-trace completeness;
- failure intelligence and failure-reuse rates;
- route diversity and route-clone pressure;
- claim calibration and a composite reliability index.

A high composite index never overrides a zero theorem-closure bit.

## Baseline comparison policy

Open-source systems are compared through pinned adapters and a common task/budget protocol. Missing measurements are represented as `null`, not zero and not an inferred score. A winner is suppressed unless every compared system has complete, comparable evidence for the selected dimensions.

The comparison dimensions include formal proof success, exact-certificate replay, claim calibration, scope honesty, counterexample yield, failure reuse, route diversity, independent verification, tool-trace completeness, public-trace completeness, cold reproducibility and cost.

## Reproduction

From `Projects/MathArc_Research`:

```bash
python -m compileall -q matharc tests scripts
python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_matharc_v02.py' -v
python -m matharc.v02 demo --out-dir artifacts/v0.2-demo
python -m matharc.v02 validate --run artifacts/v0.2-demo/run.json
python -m matharc.v02 replay --run artifacts/v0.2-demo/run.json
python scripts/v0_2_acceptance.py
```

The v0.1 acceptance contract remains in CI and must continue to pass.
